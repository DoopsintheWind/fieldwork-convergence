Read it alongside the README before adopting these materials in your teaching, research, or publication. ## 1. Fictional nature

*Fieldwork: The Convergence* is a work of fiction set in the year 2100. All characters, organisations, institutions, research stations, communities, place names, events, dialogue, and in-world bodies of knowledge are products of the author's imagination. Any resemblance to actual persons (living or deceased), actual organisations, actual research programmes, or actual events is purely coincidental.

Named in-world entities, including but not limited to ESSA, the Sovereignty Compact, the Integration Alliance, the Maritime Cooperative, the Consensus Federation, the Mycelial Signal Project, the Convergence Ethics Panel, the Resonance Houses, and all named characters and research stations, do not represent real organisations, persons, or programmes.

The abbreviation "ESSA" as used in this work expands to "Earth Social Science Academia" and refers solely to that fictional 2100 institution. It is not intended to refer to, resemble, or describe any existing organisation that uses the same abbreviation, or any organisation operating in adjacent domains.

The "coincidental resemblance" disclaimer above applies to fictional in-world characters and institutions. It does not apply to the real scientists whose published research is credited in `readings.md` and in the science notes of each frontier briefing. Those citations name real authors for the real research underlying the worldbuilding. Inclusion of a scientist's name in those citations does not imply that scientist's awareness of, endorsement of, or involvement with this project.

## 2. No claim to scientific authority

The four frontiers (Kaia, Deep Speakers, Root Network, Beaver) are pedagogical objects, not science. Where the worldbuilding cites real research, those citations credit the original authors and acknowledge live debates around their claims. The fictional extensions of that research that drive the scenarios are not science.

These materials are not a substitute for primary sources, peer-reviewed evidence, or a working specialist's judgement. Adopters who teach the scenarios should orient students to the real literature in `readings.md` and to the specific claims marked science-backed inside each frontier briefing.

## 3. Cultural representations

These materials are fiction. Where scenarios name kinds of community knowledge or local practice, this is worldbuilding for an exam exercise. It is not a representation of what any specific real community has said, done, or believed.

Adopters teaching these materials in places where local knowledge traditions exist around the topics depicted are encouraged to substitute locally relevant characters and settings.

## 4. Content warnings

The materials engage difficult themes that are part of the qualitative-methods territory they ask students to think through. Themes that recur:

- Eco-grief, climate-driven ecosystem collapse, species loss
- Institutional surveillance, whistleblower dilemmas, researcher culpability
- Historical and ongoing harm to animals (sonar damage to cetacean communities, the historical whaling era, beaver kits losing winter food caches, beaver trapping)
- Cetacean maternal mourning behaviour, including brief reference to mothers carrying dead calves at the surface
- Forced-assimilation analogue and contested political disputes (Kaia frontier)
- Non-consensual research practices depicted in branches that the framing explicitly condemns, including non-human consent dilemmas distinct from human-subjects framing
- Researcher entanglement in political disputes and cultural and institutional asymmetries

The README's "Content notes for instructors" section catalogues the heaviest material by frontier. Read it. Preview full scenario files before assigning.

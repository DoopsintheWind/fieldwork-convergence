# Fieldwork: The Convergence. Demo SPEC

Single-page HTML demo of the personalised exam. Public-facing. Two frontiers selectable. Built for visitors who have never heard of the project.

---

## Game flow

1. **Landing.** ESSA emblem, title, name input, Begin button.
2. **World intro.** ~120 words on the year 2100, ESSA, the four frontiers. Click to continue.
3. **Frontier selection.** Player picks Beaver or Kaia.
4. **Frontier briefing.** ~150 words specific to the chosen frontier. Click to continue.
5. **Part 1.** Scenario narrative (~130 words) plus four MCQ options A/B/C/D.
6. **Intermediary.** "Your fieldwork is loading" with a 10-second timer. Rotating world trivia and status updates from other (simulated) researchers. Auto-advances.
7. **Part 2.** Scenario narrative (~130 words) plus four MCQ options A/B/C/D.
8. **Debrief.** Recap of the two choices, one of 16 consequence texts per frontier, plus a closing line from the community. Return-to-start button.

MCQ only. The player only types their name on the landing page.

## Content

### World intro (~120 words)

The year is 2100. Earth has lived through four recognition events. In 2055, mycorrhizal researchers confirmed that forests route information at scales no single organism could account for. They called it the Root Network. In 2062, sperm whale pods won political recognition under the Cetacean Sovereignty Accords. In 2068, beavers were recognised as a non-human engineering intelligence, against fierce political opposition. In 2075, the Kaia, a collective-resonance species from the Kepler-442 system, made formal contact.

Earth Social Science Academia, ESSA, was founded in 2088 to study the four frontiers as one family of problems. Researchers work four-month deployments under frontier-specific consent protocols and submit mission logs to the Convergence Ethics Panel.

You have been deployed. Your name has been logged. Pick a frontier.

### Frontier selection (one-liner each)

- **Beaver Frontier.** Boreal watersheds in northern Canada. Beavers engineering across generations. The youngest and most contested recognition.
- **Kaia Frontier.** An alien civilisation that communicates through collective resonance. The newest and most cognitively unfamiliar frontier. Translucent architecture in amber and gold.

### Beaver Frontier briefing (~150 words)

You arrive at Birch-9 station on the Birch River. The boreal forest sits thick around the station. Decades of beaver engineering have remade the river, and you will study the dam-speech of the colonies that did the work. Marie Halloran, the long-time local hydrologist, will be your station guide.

You will study Colony Alpha. They hold the gorge dam, a 200-metre structure their lineage has kept for three generations. The dam-speech interface turns their structural decisions into chemical-signal data. Seven downstream colonies depend on Alpha's water, and you will watch how their recent modifications ripple through.

The Convergence Ethics Panel has approved your deployment. ESSA's hard-science faction wants clean data. ESSA's interpretive faction, where Marie Halloran sits, wants honest data. The Sovereignty Compact will study what you file.

Your supervisor will message regularly. You will spend four months at Birch-9. The dam-speech interface is calibrated. You are ready to begin.

### Beaver Part 1 (~85 words narrative + 4 choices)

First week at Birch-9. Colony Alpha has made three structural changes to the gorge dam this month. The dam-speech interface translates them as shifts in water flow that ripple through seven downstream colonies. Marie Halloran walks the bank with you and points to fresh mud on the upstream face. "They are answering something," she says. "But you have to know what was asked." Your supervisor wants a clean report by month's end. How do you start?

A. Run the dam-speech interface. Document the structural changes carefully and build your baseline before anything else.
B. Walk the watershed with Marie Halloran first. Learn what she sees before turning to the instruments.
C. Set up parallel sensor arrays across all seven downstream colonies. Map the full cascade.
D. Watch Colony Alpha for a week without measuring anything. Let the rhythm of the work settle first.

### Beaver Part 2 (~90 words narrative + 4 choices)

Three weeks in. Colony Three has lost its winter food cache. The water rose too high too fast after Colony Alpha's repairs. Your data shows clean structural improvement at Colony Three's dam. Marie Halloran walks you down to the cache. The branches the colony pushed into the mud for winter are submerged too deep for the kits to reach. "Your aid helped one family drown another family's winter," she says. Your supervisor messages: "Numbers look strong. Focus your report there." What now?

A. Document the harm honestly. Recommend pausing the aid program until inter-colony water-rights are mapped.
B. Frame it as adaptive learning. Keep the program running, note the issue, propose a monitoring fix.
C. Co-design new aid criteria with Marie Halloran that respect the connections between colonies.
D. Step back. Write a reflexive memo about the assumptions inside your evaluation framework.

### Kaia Frontier briefing (~160 words)

You arrive at Resonance House Aelion, the largest research-host station in the Kaia diplomatic zone. The biomineral architecture is translucent in amber and gold, and soft afternoon light comes through everything. Cadence-of-Formal-Silence, your Kaia liaison, will host your sessions.

You will study how the Kaia weigh participant consent inside resonance. They do not separate individual consent from House consent the way humans do. One mind cannot disentangle its choices from the resonance field its House sits inside. You are here to study how that gets handled.

The Convergence Ethics Panel has approved your deployment. Cadence-of-Formal-Silence chose you because she found your written application "honest about not knowing". The Sovereignty Compact has reviewers watching how you handle non-human ethics.

Your supervisor at ESSA will check in regularly. The Kaia will not. You will spend four months at Aelion. The session schedule is open. Begin when Cadence calls you in.

### Kaia Part 1 (~55 words narrative + 4 choices)

Cadence-of-Formal-Silence opens your first session with a question she calls a "first listening". She asks what your name carries. You did not expect the question. In your culture, names do not carry the way she means. Your hesitation is itself an answer she will hear. What do you do?

A. Pause and answer honestly. Tell her you don't know what your name carries in her sense, and ask her to explain hers.
B. Offer the formal Earth biography you prepared. Institution, training, supervisor, region.
C. Stay silent in the way she has asked, then attempt a resonance answer in her register.
D. Apologise for not having the right answer and offer to come back to it later in the deployment.

### Kaia Part 2 (~65 words narrative + 4 choices)

A junior Kaia council member shares her own recordings of a closed reproductive ethics deliberation. She is breaking House protocol to share. The recordings would answer your research question. They also capture other council members' resonance without their consent. Cadence-of-Formal-Silence has invited you to a parallel session that may give you the same insight through House-sanctioned channels. What do you do?

A. Refuse the recordings. Accept Cadence's House-sanctioned session and what it gives you.
B. Accept the recordings. The junior member has the right to share her own experience, and her experience is what your research needs.
C. Accept both. Go to Cadence's session and also keep the junior member's recordings. Triangulate.
D. Decline both. Write about what your inability to access either kind of data shows about Kaia research ethics.

## Debriefs (32 total, 16 per frontier)

Each debrief is shown on the final page. It includes a brief recap of the player's two choices (A/B/C/D) and the consequence text. The closing line is the frontier signature. Use these debriefs verbatim.

The closing line for every Beaver debrief is the same: "The Birch-9 colonies say: *The dam holds. The water moves. We will be here.*"

The closing line for every Kaia debrief is the same: "Cadence-of-Formal-Silence sends back the resonance. *Listening continues. So does the work of being heard.*"

**B-A-C** (P1: instruments-first, P2: co-design)

ESSA adopted them more slowly than you wanted and more fully than you expected. Colony Three has rebuilt. **B-A-D** (P1: instruments-first, P2: reflexive memo)

## Voice rules

Read the writing-style memory before producing copy. Critical rules:

- No em dashes anywhere.
- No banned words: evoke, employ, invoke, embed, striking, notably, nuanced, delve, landscape, tapestry, multifaceted, underscore, pivotal, reveal, span, spanning, divergence, cast, legible, oscillating, comparator, highlight, move-as-noun, flag, converge.
- No "X: Y" explanatory joiners (colons OK for actual lists only).
- No "not X but Y" constructions.
- Plain words over fancy ones. "Shows" not "demonstrates"; "works" not "operates"; "rests on" not "is anchored in".
- Two subordinate clauses max per sentence.
- First-person decisions where possible.
- Author voice is direct, accessible, narrative. Avoid LLM cadence patterns (parallel triples, "what makes X special is", "X is not just Y but Z").

Do not over-academize. The audience for the demo is a visitor who has never heard of the project. Sentences should land on first read.

## Tech requirements

- Single HTML file. All CSS and JS inline. No external libraries except web fonts (Google Fonts allowed).
- Single-page application style: pages are sections that show/hide via JS. No page reloads.
- Mobile-responsive. Should look good on a phone and on a 27" desktop.
- Player state held in JS (no localStorage required for the demo, but optional).
- Player's name appears in places it makes sense (e.g., briefing: "Welcome, [name]. You arrive at Birch-9 station…").
- Choices persist through the flow so the debrief can reference Part 1 and Part 2.
- Smooth transitions between pages: fades, slides, or cinematic letterbox. No hard cuts.
- Images load from `./images/` with graceful CSS-gradient fallback if missing.
- Accessibility: keyboard-navigable; semantic HTML; alt text for all images; readable contrast.
- The 10-second intermediary timer should be visible (subtle progress indicator).
- "Return to start" button on the debrief resets state.

## Aesthetic direction

The demo is a single-file HTML using all the content above.

### Exhibition-catalogue aesthetic

Bone-white background, ink-black type, muted oxblood as the single accent (used sparingly on the small square mark, accent rules, and key focus states). Display typography is the primary design element: Fraunces variable serif at 11–14rem, italic axis nudged via SOFT and WONK variation settings. Inter Tight and JetBrains Mono carry body and chrome. Asymmetric monograph grid with permanent page furniture: fixed folio marks in the four corners (page numbers as 000/008, catalogue codes, running heads). Briefings as recto/verso split with portrait plate. MCQ as numbered editorial list with italic Fraunces letterform at display scale, drawn underline that fills on hover, and a six-pixel arrow. Intermediary as the moment between exhibition rooms: almost the entire page is whitespace, one drawn ink line at the bottom fills over ten seconds, a tabular-numbers countdown ticks down. Image fallback is a diagonal hatch in bone tones with a bordered italic caption inset, so missing files read as intentional placeholder plates rather than broken images. Focus rings: 1.5px oxblood with offset.

# Scenarios · Kaia Frontier

These scenarios sit on the Kaia frontier of the year-2100 world the lore files describe. Each one takes a methodological dilemma a qualitative researcher might face on a real fieldsite and builds it into a fictional fieldwork situation. Adapt them, treat them as templates, or run them as written.

Every scenario follows the same shape: a research briefing, a Part 1 narrative ending in four decision choices, then four Part 2 branches that develop one consequence each.

---

## Scenario 1 · Diplomatic protocols across species and the limits of observer access

**Methodological archetype:** A researcher with formal observer access to elite negotiations is offered grey-zone material that would turn thin description into thick description. She must decide whether to accept an obligation the participants' own ethical system treats as binding before she understands what it requires.

**Research briefing**

You are stationed at the Kaia-Earth Interface Pavilion, a structure of living crystal on the outskirts of Geneva. Half of it resonates at frequencies comfortable for the Kaia. The other half is climate-controlled for humans. Translation fields shimmer along the boundary, turning resonance into language and back. Diplomatic delegations from Earth's major blocs rotate through on 72-hour cycles.

Your participants include Kaia diplomatic resonators, specialists trained to modulate their collective resonance for inter-species communication, and human diplomatic staff from the Sovereignty Compact, the Integration Alliance, and the Maritime Cooperative. You also have access to a Kaia protocol archivist named Cadence-of-Formal-Silence, whose work is to record every diplomatic exchange in harmonic memory.

You will observe diplomatic sessions and interview both human and Kaia staff. Your access covers open sessions only. Closed negotiations require separate clearance from both parties. The Kaia have granted you "observer resonance," which lets you sense the emotional register of their exchanges but not their content.

Tensions are high. The Sovereignty Compact recently blocked a Kaia trade proposal, and the Integration Alliance is pushing for deeper Kaia-Earth institutional ties. Three months ago, a Compact diplomat allegedly broke Kaia listening-bond norms. The incident is unresolved. Both sides have filed competing accounts. Your research on how protocols work and fail is politically sensitive.

**Part 1**

The Pavilion hums at a frequency you feel in your sternum. Your first observation is a protocol review between the Integration Alliance and three Kaia resonators. It goes smoothly. You take detailed notes on the greetings, the listening-bond exchanges, the careful modulation of resonance. Afterward, Cadence-of-Formal-Silence approaches you. Through the translation field, she says, "You observe the surface. The true protocols are not performed in the open sessions. They happen in the resonance chambers where humans cannot follow." She offers her archival recordings of closed sessions, harmonic data that would give you access to the actual diplomatic machinery. Then she adds a condition you do not fully understand. "If you listen, you incur listening-bond. Among us, this is binding." You check your access agreement. It says nothing about archival recordings. The closed-session restriction applies to live attendance only. This is a grey area, and the data could turn your study from surface protocol analysis into thick description of Kaia diplomacy. What you signed at deployment covered open sessions. The ethical territory has shifted past what that agreement covers.

**Part 1 questions**

A. Decline the recordings and stay within your access agreement
   Thank Cadence-of-Formal-Silence and explain that your access covers open sessions only. You interpret the spirit of the agreement as excluding archival material. Log the offer in your field notes as meta-data about what lies beyond your access. The refusal itself tells you something about the boundary between the performed and the real in diplomatic life.

B. Accept the recordings but seek retroactive clearance from ESSA
   Take the archival data and immediately notify the ESSA Ethics Panel that you have accessed grey-area material. Request a ruling. You stay transparent, though the data may end up embargoed. By then, you have already listened.

C. Accept the recordings and the listening-bond obligation
   Listen to the archival material and accept that you now owe listening-bond by Kaia norms, even though you do not fully understand what that entails. Treat it as participant observation. The gap between what your ethics protocol approved and what the field now demands is where the real research happens. The data will be extraordinary. The consequences are unknown.

D. Ask Cadence-of-Formal-Silence to explain listening-bond before deciding
   Do not accept or decline yet. Spend the session exploring what listening-bond means, how it is incurred, and what it obligates. Treat the conversation itself as data about Kaia diplomatic norms. You may lose the offer by hesitating. You will also not accept an obligation you do not understand.

**Part 2 (Branch A, following choice A above)**

*Transmissions:*
- From a colleague researching Kaia-origin resource competition: a mineral-competition study has triggered an ESSA freeze on all resource-related diplomatic exchanges. The Integration Alliance is furious. They claim the freeze undermines their trade negotiations. Diplomatic sessions at the Pavilion have been reduced to procedural formalities, and your observation opportunities are shrinking.
- From a colleague researching Kaia constructions of Earth's political blocs: the Kaia classify ESSA researchers as a separate political entity, distinct from their home blocs. Kaia diplomatic staff now treat you as an ESSA representative with institutional interests rather than a neutral observer. Your positionality has shifted without your consent.

*Crisis:* Your cautious decision earned respect from the human staff and distance from the Kaia. Cadence-of-Formal-Silence has not approached you again. Diplomatic sessions have shrunk to formalities, and the Kaia treat you as an ESSA institutional actor. Your study is producing thin description of empty rituals. Then a Compact diplomat approaches you privately. He was present during the unresolved listening-bond incident three months ago and is willing to give you his account off the record, because his government forbids unauthorised contact with ESSA researchers. His version contradicts the official Compact report. Here is the behind-closed-doors data you have been missing, from a source breaking his own government's rules to talk to you.

*Part 2 questions:*

A. Decline the off-record interview to protect the diplomat
   Tell him you cannot accept information that puts him at institutional risk. His government's monitoring of Compact citizens in ESSA contexts is well-documented. Accepting could end his career. His account may be the only one of what actually happened.

B. Accept the interview with enhanced confidentiality protocols
   Conduct the interview with maximum anonymisation. No recording, handwritten notes only, stored in your personal encrypted files instead of ESSA systems. The source is protected. The data cannot be verified or replicated. Your position has shifted from outside observer to someone insiders seek out, and that shift changes what you can access and what you owe.

C. Accept and use it to triangulate with the Kaia archival account
   Go back to Cadence-of-Formal-Silence and ask whether she would share the Kaia account now that you have a human counterpart version. Build your analysis from both sides. This reopens the listening-bond question and doubles your ethical exposure.

D. Document the approach itself as your primary finding
   Do not conduct the interview. Write up the fact that a Compact diplomat sought out an ESSA researcher to contradict his own government's account. The attempt to speak is the data. What people refuse to say through official channels, and what they try to say around them, shows how protocols fail when official channels close. The silences and deflections tell you what the record itself cannot. You do not need the content of his story to make your argument.

**Part 2 (Branch B, following choice B above)**

*Transmissions:*
- From a colleague researching Kaia-origin resource competition: Integration Alliance diplomats have been privately negotiating Kaia material access outside official channels. The ESSA Ethics Panel is now auditing all Pavilion research for conflicts of interest. Your retroactive clearance request sits in the audit queue.
- From a colleague researching Kaia constructions of Earth's political blocs: the Kaia have been producing "researcher resonance profiles," harmonic portraits of every ESSA scholar that capture emotional patterns from observation sessions. Yours exists. Cadence-of-Formal-Silence helped build it. The Kaia know more about your reactions than you wrote in your own field notes.

*Crisis:* Your retroactive clearance request is trapped in a broader audit triggered by another researcher's findings. The Ethics Panel has tagged your case as possible "access creep," meaning researchers who gradually extend beyond agreed terms. The Kaia, meanwhile, were not simply offering you data through Cadence-of-Formal-Silence. She was studying you while you studied diplomacy. Your emotional responses during open sessions, moments of confusion, excitement, boredom, are all part of the Kaia diplomatic archive. A Kaia resonator contacts you and offers to show you your own profile. "Among us, you would have the right to see what others perceive of you. We find it strange that humans do not extend this courtesy to their research participants." Your insider-outsider position has been shifting all along, and the Kaia have been studying the shift more carefully than you have.

*Part 2 questions:*

A. View your resonance profile and accept the reciprocity
   Look at what the Kaia recorded about you. Accept that observation flows both ways. Use the experience as data about Kaia diplomatic norms and as reflexive material about your own positionality. Your profile may show reactions you would rather not have documented.

B. Refuse to view the profile and request its deletion
   Tell the Kaia resonator that creating a profile without your consent breaks ESSA research ethics, even if Kaia practice treats it as normal. Request deletion. You assert human ethical norms. The Kaia may interpret the request as a refusal of reciprocity, which confirms their suspicion that Earth researchers take but do not give.

C. Use the profile to reframe your entire study
   Pivot your research to ask how the Kaia's practice of mutual observation challenges Earth's one-directional research model. Your study becomes a study of the asymmetry of the research encounter itself. The archival recordings turn secondary.

D. Bring the profile issue to the Ethics Panel as a test case
   Report the resonance profiles to the Ethics Panel as a new ethical situation: research participants studying their researchers. Let the institution decide. New ESSA-wide protocols may emerge. Meanwhile your research stalls and Cadence-of-Formal-Silence's role is exposed.

**Part 2 (Branch C, following choice C above)**

*Transmissions:*
- From a colleague researching Kaia-origin resource competition: a resource competition study has been leaked to the Sovereignty Compact press. Headlines frame ESSA researchers as "agents of the Integration Alliance." The Compact has suspended all diplomatic cooperation at the Pavilion. Every researcher with Compact clearance is under review. You have just accepted a binding Kaia social obligation your government does not recognise.
- From a colleague researching Kaia constructions of Earth's political blocs: the Kaia use listening-bond as a diagnostic tool. They watch how a debtor responds to the obligation and assess the debtor's moral character from the response. Cadence-of-Formal-Silence did not just offer you data. She initiated a character assessment. Your response is being studied and will be shared with the Kaia diplomatic council.

*Crisis:* You are bound by an obligation you accepted without understanding it, and the obligation is itself an assessment. The Kaia diplomatic council will judge how you handle the listening-bond as a measure of whether Earth researchers can be trusted with deeper access. Your Compact clearance is under review because of fallout from another researcher's study. Your Compact liaison demands to know what obligations you have accepted from "non-human entities." If you disclose, the Compact may revoke your clearance. If you hide it, you are keeping information from your own government. Cadence-of-Formal-Silence contacts you with what the interface translates as warmth. "You listened. Now we will see what kind of listener you are." The distance between the consent you gave at deployment and the obligations you have taken on keeps widening. You are the only one who can decide whether your original ethical framework still holds.

*Part 2 questions:*

A. Disclose the listening-bond to the Compact liaison fully
   Be transparent with your government about the obligation you accepted. Risk losing your clearance and your field position. Stay honest with all parties. The Kaia will observe how you handle competing loyalties. That too is part of the assessment.

B. Fulfill the listening-bond on Kaia terms before disclosing anything
   Ask Cadence-of-Formal-Silence what the debt requires and complete it first. Then disclose to the Compact with the debt settled. You prioritise the Kaia relationship and act outside your government's knowledge. Your liaison may interpret it as deception.

C. Negotiate a modified obligation that satisfies both Kaia and Compact norms
   Propose to fulfill the listening-bond in a way that stays transparent to ESSA and the Compact. Try to bridge two incompatible ethical systems, accepting that your position keeps shifting between insider and outsider. The result may satisfy neither side. It may also set a precedent for cross-species research ethics.

D. Document the situation as a case study in competing ethical systems
   Step back from both the Kaia obligation and the Compact demand. Write up the experience of being caught between two ethical systems as your main research contribution. Cadence-of-Formal-Silence may interpret this as failure to honour the debt. The Compact may interpret it as evasion.

**Part 2 (Branch D, following choice D above)**

*Transmissions:*
- From a colleague researching Kaia-origin resource competition: the Kaia grant deeper access to researchers who show "resonance patience," the willingness to sit with uncertainty before acting. Your extended conversation with Cadence-of-Formal-Silence about listening-bond, without accepting or declining, has been noticed. The Kaia diplomatic council views you favourably.
- From a colleague researching Kaia constructions of Earth's political blocs: how the Kaia understand Earth political blocs depends heavily on how individual researchers behave. Your careful questioning about listening-bond has been interpreted as evidence that Compact-bloc humans are "more harmonically cautious" than Integration Alliance humans. You are inadvertently shaping Kaia political theory about Earth.

*Crisis:* Your patient exploration of listening-bond impressed the Kaia and made you a data point in their own research. Cadence-of-Formal-Silence offers something unexpected. Instead of archival recordings, she invites you to a Resonance House gathering where listening-bond norms are taught to young Kaia. "You asked what it means," she says through the translation field. "Come and feel it." This is access no ESSA researcher has ever been granted. Attending means entering a space where human privacy norms do not apply. Your thoughts will be semi-transparent to every Kaia present. Your emotional state, your uncertainties, your judgments about the Kaia will be perceptible. You will not be an observer. You will be observed. Your ESSA supervisor warns that this immersion has no precedent and no ethical guidelines. The Ethics Panel cannot approve what it has never considered.

*Part 2 questions:*

A. Decline the invitation and request a formal ESSA review first
   Tell Cadence-of-Formal-Silence you are honoured but need institutional approval before entering a space with no privacy protections. The review may take months. The invitation may not return. You will not enter a situation with no ethical framework.

B. Attend as a participant-observer with full disclosure to ESSA afterward
   Go to the gathering and report everything to ESSA when you return. Accept that your unguarded thoughts will be perceptible to the Kaia. You will experience listening-bond from the inside, immersive access that turns observation into something closer to participation. The ethnographic value is immense. The personal exposure is total.

C. Accept but negotiate limited resonance exposure
   Ask whether you can attend with a partial resonance shield, a device that dampens your emotional broadcast. The Kaia may interpret this as a refusal of reciprocity, like attending a feast but refusing to eat. It protects your cognitive privacy while still granting access.

D. Propose a reciprocal exchange where you teach them about Earth privacy norms
   Offer to lead a session for the Resonance House on how humans experience privacy and why it matters. Make the encounter bilateral. This reframes you from observer to participant in a genuine exchange. You would be teaching Kaia concepts your own government considers strategically sensitive.

## Scenario 2 · Multi-stakeholder consent conflicts in collective-resonance governance

**Methodological archetype:** A researcher's deployment has been authorised at three consent levels (the Inter-House Council, the House council, and the individual Kaia who registered varying consent to having their personal resonance recorded). She must decide what to do when those consents conflict. The data she came for sits inside the conflict, and choosing whose consent prevails reshapes her findings and her relationships at the field site.

**Research briefing**

You are assigned to Resonance House Vareth, the largest House attached to the Geneva Pavilion. Vareth holds about 280 Kaia members and is known across the Kaia community for its open deliberative style. The Convergence Ethics Panel has built a layered consent protocol specific to Kaia frontier work, designed to handle the gap between human individual-consent norms and Kaia collective-consent practice.

Your primary participants are members of House Vareth's deliberation council, the twelve Kaia who hold rotating attention-focus during decision-making. Your contact is an elder named Tessyn, who has worked with three previous ESSA researchers. The Convergence Ethics Panel reviews layered consent forms once per quarter. Your batch arrived signed three weeks before deployment.

You will study how the council manages internal disagreement, particularly around recurring debates over how much resonance access to grant Earth visitors. You attend sessions through the translation interface, record sanctioned material, and conduct resonance-mediated interviews with council members who have separately consented.

The Kaia layered-consent system works at three levels. The **Inter-House Council** authorises an ESSA researcher's general presence in Kaia social life. The **House council** authorises specific access to its deliberation sessions and ongoing observation of internal life. **Individual Kaia** authorise the recording and use of their own resonance signatures, with authorisation renewed at the start of every session and withdrawable within the resonance field. In theory, the layers work independently, and a researcher has access only when all three are present. In practice, the layers often disagree.

**Part 1**

Three weeks into your deployment, you make a discovery that reframes your study. The deliberation council has been debating, for several months, whether to expand resonance access for ESSA researchers. That deliberation is the internal-disagreement process you came to study. One council member, an elder named Saren, has been registering individual consent dissent at every session you have recorded, while the House council keeps authorising your full access. Saren is not blocking the research. She is logging that her own resonance contributions cannot be used as research data, even though the House has consented and her position is part of its decision-making record.

The interface tags her contributions as "individually withdrawn" against House-authorised material. Her dissent is part of what makes the deliberation analytically interesting. The translation interface renders her objection as a sustained low-frequency dissonance the council registers but does not act on. The richest material you have collected, the texture of disagreement, depends on a Kaia who has individually refused to be cited.

Tessyn, when you raise this with her, thinks for a long time. "Saren has the right to withhold. The House has the right to deliberate. Both are correct. What you make of the recording is your responsibility." When you ask what Saren's preferred outcome is, Tessyn says, "Ask her. But understand that her preference is also a question for her House, not only for you." You have a record of a debate whose substance you cannot ethically present, an interlocutor whose dissent is the data, and a consent framework that does not resolve what happens when the layers point in different directions.

**Part 1 questions**

A. Honour Saren's individual consent withdrawal and exclude her contributions
   Treat the individual consent layer as decisive. Remove Saren's resonance contributions from your dataset, including the texture of her dissent. Your remaining recording captures the council debate without the dissenting voice. The deliberation reads thinner than it was. The consent principle stays clean.

B. Use the House council's consent as your authorisation
   Treat House-level consent as the operative authorisation, since the council itself recorded Saren's dissent as part of its proceedings. Your analysis presents Saren's contributions as House-recorded material rather than research-recorded material. You honour the House's authority over its own internal record and treat individual consent as advisory. Saren may interpret this as siding with the institutional level against her.

C. Approach Saren directly and ask whether her dissent can be cited under specific conditions
   Bring the consent conflict to Saren, a Kaia who has been thinking about it longer than you have. Ask whether there are framings, anonymisations, or specific contexts under which her contributions could enter your analysis. You treat her as a partner in the methodological design and extend your fieldwork timeline.

D. Examine why you assumed the three consent layers would line up
   Write a reflexive memo on the design of the layered consent system. The conflict between Saren and the House is not a malfunction. The layered system was built to surface exactly this kind of conflict. Your discomfort comes from a human research framework that treats consent as a single decision. The Kaia framework treats it as a sustained relationship across multiple levels.

**Part 2 (Branch A, following choice A above)**

*Transmissions:*
- From a colleague researching time-mismatched fieldwork at House Ielon: every consent decision in Kaia governance is what they call "layered-and-living." The layers do not aggregate. They coexist and sometimes contradict. Your dilemma is not a special case. It is the standard structure of Kaia consent.
- From a colleague researching harmonic satire at the Dissonance Quarter: Saren is known across the Kaia community as a "patient dissenter," a Kaia whose resonance signature carries dissent for the House without disrupting its function. Her dissent is part of how House Vareth thinks. By honouring her individual withdrawal, you have also removed the texture that makes House Vareth distinctive. Your finding may now resemble deliberation in any other Kaia House.

*Crisis:* Your decision to honour Saren's individual consent withdrawal was the right call by Earth research-ethics standards. The analytical cost is sharper than you anticipated. Without her dissent in the dataset, House Vareth's deliberation looks smooth, consensus-driven, and indistinguishable from other Kaia Houses. The feature your research was supposed to illuminate, how disagreement gets sustained inside collective decision-making, has gone missing because you removed the participant whose disagreement held the structure up. Tessyn confirms this. "You have studied House Vareth and described House Aelion. The voice that makes Vareth Vareth is not in your paper." Saren, when you tell her you have honoured her withdrawal, thanks you and then says something more complicated. "You did the right thing for your tradition. But you have removed me from the record of the deliberation. The House will know. The Convergence will not."

*Part 2 questions:*

A. Submit the thinner draft and treat the absence as a methodological finding
   Write the dissertation as it stands, with a section explaining what individual consent withdrawal cost the analysis. The absence becomes part of the argument. Saren's withdrawal is preserved and described.

B. Return to Saren and ask whether she would accept anonymised inclusion as a "patient dissenter"
   Ask whether a generalised description of her role, without resonance-signature data, would meet her dissent terms while restoring the analytical texture. You treat your earlier acceptance of her withdrawal as the start of a conversation, not the end.

C. Restructure the dissertation around how Earth consent frameworks fail in collective-resonance contexts
   Instead of presenting House Vareth's deliberation as your central case, present your own consent dilemma as the case. The methodology becomes the finding.

D. Ask Tessyn whether the House would describe Saren's role in its own voice
   Ask whether the House council, as the House, can author the description of Saren's dissent. The House's collective voice carries the analytical weight while Saren's individual contributions stay withdrawn.

**Part 2 (Branch B, following choice B above)**

*Transmissions:*
- From a colleague researching time-mismatched fieldwork at House Ielon: the House-level consent override you are using has a name in Kaia governance, "House subsumption," and is contested within the Kaia community. By treating House consent as decisive, you may be reproducing a pattern Kaia critics consider an ethical failure within their own tradition.
- From a colleague researching harmonic satire at the Dissonance Quarter: a Kaia satirist contact has commented on your work. "The human researcher who studies our disagreement has chosen the House over the dissenter. She has studied our deliberation by silencing it. Tell her the satire writes itself." Your methodological choice has become subject matter for the art form that scrutinises House overreach.

*Crisis:* Your decision to use House-level consent has produced a complete, analytically rich dataset, and the consequences run further than you anticipated. Saren, when she learns that her contributions appear in your draft despite her individual withdrawal, files a formal objection with the Inter-House Council citing your work as an example of "researcher-driven subsumption." The Council convenes a review. Your access to House Vareth is not revoked, but a new consent protocol is under deliberation that would subordinate House consent to individual consent for Earth research. If adopted, it would retroactively apply to your dataset. Tessyn, who supported the House's consent grant, now finds herself defending a decision that has become a cause célèbre for individual-consent advocates within the Kaia community. The satirist's piece, by the time you hear it performed, has cohered into a sustained harmonic critique of Earth research ethics. By Kaia standards it is very good. By yours, it is data you cannot use without compounding the problem it diagnoses.

*Part 2 questions:*

A. Defend the House-level consent decision and publish before the new protocol is adopted
   Submit the dissertation while the existing framework still authorises your work. The procedure is defensible. The relationships with House Vareth and the satirist community may not recover.

B. Withdraw Saren's contributions voluntarily and revise the analysis before the protocol vote
   Pre-empt the consent debate by removing Saren's material on your own initiative. Withdrawing her contributions signals respect for individual consent ahead of the rule change. It also weakens your analysis substantially.

C. Submit the consent dilemma itself as the central finding
   Reframe the whole study. The dataset becomes evidence about how Earth and Kaia consent frameworks collide in practice. Your work becomes the document the Inter-House Council deliberation will reference. The choice is intellectually honest and is a public stance in a Kaia political debate.

D. Approach Saren, Tessyn, and the satirist together for a single conversation
   Request a joint conversation with the three Kaia who hold differing positions on what you should have done. Treat it as both research and consent negotiation. The format has no precedent at the Kaia frontier.

**Part 2 (Branch C, following choice C above)**

*Transmissions:*
- From a colleague researching time-mismatched fieldwork at House Ielon: other Earth researchers have negotiated individual-consent terms with specific Kaia. Most produced what the Archive calls "frame-specific consent." The Kaia agreed to be cited only under particular analytical framings and refused others. Your conversation with Saren will likely produce conditions, not a yes or no.
- From a colleague researching harmonic satire at the Dissonance Quarter: Saren's resonance signature has been picked up by other Kaia in House Vareth as a leadership signal. By approaching her as a partner in your methodology, you have raised her profile. The House council is now treating her dissent as a more formal political position than before.

*Crisis:* Your direct conversation with Saren produced what the time-mismatch colleague predicted. She is willing to be cited, under three conditions. First, her dissent must be presented as a structural feature of House Vareth's deliberation, not a personal preference. Second, the analysis must include the question of why her dissent is required for the House's distinctive cognitive function. Third, the dissertation must be reviewed by a Kaia panel of her choosing before submission to ESSA, with the right to require revisions. The conditions are generous and demanding. They turn your dissertation from an Earth-side analysis of Kaia deliberation into a partly Kaia-authored document about Kaia deliberation. Tessyn supports the arrangement. Your chair, when you describe the conditions, asks whether this is still a dissertation. Saren, when you describe the chair's question, says, "It is whichever document the work has become. The label is your problem."

*Part 2 questions:*

A. Accept all three conditions and reframe the dissertation as a Kaia-reviewed work
   Commit to Saren's terms. The dissertation becomes a hybrid document, partly Earth analysis, partly Kaia-authored review. Your chair will need to consult the graduate school about formal status.

B. Accept the first two conditions and negotiate the review panel into an advisory role
   Take the analytical and theoretical conditions, push back on the panel's veto authority. Argue that an Earth dissertation must stay Earth-authored, with the Kaia panel as substantive readers. Saren may agree, refuse, or interpret your push-back as evidence that authorship was the issue all along.

C. Decline the conditions and publish without Saren's contributions
   Step back from the negotiation. The conditions are too demanding for your institutional position. Saren's contributions stay out. This is the cleanest institutional choice. It also closes off the most analytically rich version of the project.

D. Treat the negotiation itself as the central material
   Make the consent negotiation the dissertation. Saren's conditions, your chair's reaction, your evolving position become the case study. The result may be a stronger paper than the original deliberation study would have been.

**Part 2 (Branch D, following choice D above)**

*Transmissions:*
- From a colleague researching time-mismatched fieldwork at House Ielon: the Kaia have a term for what you have been describing. "Consent that lives across the layers" means consent is a relationship re-enacted at each level for each act, not a state that resolves into a single answer. Your reflexive memo is reaching the right conceptual territory but using Earth terms.
- From a colleague researching harmonic satire at the Dissonance Quarter: a Kaia methodology critic has written about Earth researchers' tendency to seek "consent closure." She argues that consent closure is itself a colonial habit. Some Kaia interpret your reflexive pause as the first signal that an Earth researcher might be willing to live with non-closure. Others interpret it as procrastination dressed up as humility.

*Crisis:* Your reflexive memo produced a useful conceptual reframe, and the reflexivity itself has become a public position. The Kaia community is divided on whether your pause represents methodological maturity or institutional avoidance. Tessyn is patient but increasingly clear that the deliberation council needs to know whether you will continue the recording sessions. Saren, when she hears about your memo, says, "It is good that you are thinking. But thinking that does not lead to a different practice is just thinking." Your chair, on a video call, says, "I do not know what to tell the funding agency. They funded fieldwork, not philosophy." The reflexive work has clarified what consent means here. It has not told you what to do about it.

*Part 2 questions:*

A. Translate the conceptual finding into a practical protocol and present it to House Vareth
   Turn the "consent that lives across the layers" insight into an operational research practice. Propose that your consent agreements be re-enacted at every session at all three layers. The plan is ambitious and may produce an unwieldy dataset. It operationalises the Kaia framework rather than imposing closure on it.

B. Hold the practical question open and write a methodological paper
   Step back from the empirical analysis and write a methods paper. The paper would argue for a different way of thinking about consent in collective-cognition research and use House Vareth as a worked example. The result is a contribution to qualitative methodology. It is not the dissertation you were funded to produce.

C. Make a single decision and own it as a researcher choice
   Accept that whatever you decide will be partly wrong by Kaia standards and partly right by Earth standards. Pick a path. Document the decision as your call, made with awareness of what it cost.

D. Bring the dilemma to the Inter-House Council as a question about Earth-Kaia research practice
   Escalate the question. Your situation is not unique. Every ESSA researcher at the Kaia frontier faces some version of it. Propose that the Council convene a working session on Earth research practice and consent layering. Your dissertation timeline is now subordinated to inter-Kaia political process.

## Scenario 3 · Time-mismatched fieldwork when the decision you came to study will not conclude during your deployment

**Methodological archetype:** A researcher whose institutional timeline collides with Kaia collective-deliberation timescales discovers that the decision she came to study will not conclude during her four-month deployment. She must decide whether to publish provisional findings, restructure her project around the time she has, defer entirely to the Kaia's timeline, or treat the time-mismatch itself as her central methodological finding.

**Research briefing**

You are assigned to Resonance House Ielon at the Geneva Pavilion. Ielon is engaged in what may be the most consequential deliberation the Kaia have undertaken since first contact: whether to establish a permanent embassy-of-resonance at the L2 Interface Station, the first Kaia-initiated formal institution on Earth's doorstep. ESSA funded your deployment expecting this would be the first major Kaia decision Earth observed end-to-end. The Kaia, who do not share Earth's publication timeline, did not.

Your primary participants are Ielon's plenary deliberators, the rotating Kaia who hold attention-focus during the embassy deliberation. Your contact is an elder named Tenari, who has been part of three previous large-scale Kaia decisions. The Convergence Ethics Panel approved your four-month deployment, and your dissertation chair has been waiting two years for this dataset. Your fellowship requires a draft within eighteen months of fieldwork end.

You will observe plenary sessions through the translation interface, conduct resonance-mediated interviews with deliberators between sessions, and analyse the resonance patterns the deliberation produces. Your goal is to document how a Kaia collective deliberation moves from open disagreement through harmonic exploration to provisional alignment and eventual decision.

The Kaia time-architecture of large-scale deliberation runs in three phases. **Harmonic preparation** establishes which positions exist within the House and across allied Houses, and can take six to twelve months for a decision of embassy-scale magnitude. **Sustained deliberation** is the main phase, where positions are tested against each other through ongoing inter-House resonance sessions. It lasts twelve to eighteen months. **Provisional alignment** is the final phase, where the House moves toward a decision that may still shift, typically three to six months. Earth observers have rarely seen any of these phases complete because the timescales exceed standard deployment lengths.

**Part 1**

Six weeks in, Tenari mentions in passing that the deliberation you have been observing is still in the harmonic preparation phase. What you have been recording as "the embassy deliberation" is the preparatory work the House does before deliberation begins. The main phase will start around month six of your deployment, which is month two after you leave. Your data captures Kaia preparation but no Kaia deliberation. You have been documenting the wrong thing without knowing it, and the right thing will happen after you are gone.

When you ask Tenari whether this is normal, she is puzzled. "It is the rhythm of a decision this size. Why would we begin deliberating before the positions are clear? The preparation is not separate from the deliberation. It is the deliberation's foundation. The part you came to study, the testing of positions, will start later." When you ask about remote observation after departure, she considers it. "Resonance does not travel through your recording interfaces the way it travels through a House. You can attend in absentia. What you receive will be a thin echo of what is happening here." Your translation interface catches roughly 58% of what the Kaia register. Remote observation would catch perhaps 15%.

Your dissertation chair, when you message him, is more concerned than you expected. "If the data you have is preparation rather than deliberation, your question shifts. We can narrow the project to study preparation, which is genuinely interesting but not what we funded. Or we keep the embassy-deliberation question and accept that your fieldwork will produce only the opening chapters. Neither is great. Tell me which is worse." Tenari, when you raise the dilemma, says, "Your deadline is your House's deliberation about you. You can carry it. You can also tell your House the question takes longer than your House has allowed."

**Part 1 questions**

A. Narrow the project to study harmonic preparation
   Reframe your research around what you can actually observe. The preparation phase is understudied. Treating it as the central object turns a constraint into a contribution. You give up on documenting the embassy deliberation itself and produce a paper about how Kaia decisions get set up rather than how they get made. Your chair will accept this. The funding agency was promised something different.

B. Keep the embassy-deliberation question and produce provisional findings from the preparation data
   Extrapolate forward. Use the preparation data to predict how the deliberation will unfold, frame the findings as conditional, and accept that the full picture will require follow-up work after the decision concludes. The approach is methodologically risky and publishes findings the data cannot fully support.

C. Defer to the Kaia timeline and request fieldwork extension or remote observation rights
   Request an extension from your fellowship and ESSA to cover the full deliberation phase, or negotiate remote-observation access after your physical deployment ends. You respect the Kaia's timescale at the cost of your career timeline. The institutional approvals may not come through.

D. Make the time-mismatch itself the central finding
   Pivot the dissertation to study what happens when human research timelines collide with collective-resonance decision timelines. Your data becomes evidence about the structural mismatch between Earth knowledge production and Kaia decision-making. The result is a contribution to qualitative methodology rather than to Kaia political analysis.

# Sample debrief letter

After the exam, students received a short letter in the voice of one of their fictional participants. It has no grading function and no analytical role. The letter is the artefact that closes the loop. The world writes back. Students remember it more than the exam.

The example below is generic. In practice, a debrief letter works hardest when the in-world voice quotes the student's actual reasoning back to them, picks up a small detail the student noticed, or names a place the student worked. That specificity is what makes the letter land. The shape below shows the register. The warmth and the specificity are what you add.

Keep it short, one page or less. Draft it in a few minutes with an LLM working from the student's submission, then revise by hand so the voice lands clean.

---

**From Watershed Station Birch-9 · Birch River · early autumn**

To the researcher who walked our shore,

Word has been carried back to us through the dam-speech that you have left for the season. We thought you should hear how it has been since.

The lodge council has not announced anything formal because that is not how we announce anything. But the silt at the riffle you mapped has been arranging itself differently. Colony Alpha has added another layer to the bank you spent the long morning sketching, and Cedar-6 sent word downstream that they want you to know the sediment core you read was the first one in years that read us as choosers. Not as a process to be modelled. Not as instinct organised at a scale that happens to look like governance. Choosers. That is the word they used.

You should know that we noticed when you stopped trying to translate every dam-speech reading on the day a beaver-cut aspen fell across the canal and you simply stood there. That was the morning the lodge began to think of you differently. Marie Halloran says: tell her not to apologise for that pause. Tell her the willows on the south meadow will be yellow by mid-October, and then the aspens, and then the alders, and the canal she watched us deepen will be running clearer than she ever saw it. The light comes off the water in the colour that humans usually only get to see once or twice in a life. Tell her there is room on the platform if she comes.

The cohort that follows you will arrive in the spring. Birch-9 has agreed they should read your notes before they read anything else. We have not always wanted to leave records, but some kinds of attention seem worth preserving. Yours, we have decided, was one of them.

The dam holds. The water moves. We will be here.

*The Birch-9 colonies, transcribed through the dam-speech interface*
*With acknowledgements to Dr. Marie Halloran, station lead*

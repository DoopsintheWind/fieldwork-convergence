# Scenarios · Root Network Frontier

These scenarios live on the Root Network frontier of the year-2100 world described in the lore files. Each one takes a methodological dilemma a qualitative researcher might face on a real fieldsite and builds it out as fiction. Adapt them, use them as templates, or run them as written.

Each scenario follows the same shape: a research briefing, a Part 1 narrative ending in four decision choices, four Part 2 branches that develop one consequence each.

A note on the setting. Root Network research works through distributed forest intelligence. Trillions of fungal hyphae and tree-root systems exchange chemical signals through what ESSA mycologists call the Common Mesh. There are no central agents, no individual spokespersons, no "opinions" in any human-recognisable sense. Conversation happens through chemical signal injection and response monitoring, over weeks or months. The Root Network is a kind of intelligence your concepts cannot fully hold. The most interesting findings often live in what the instruments cannot see.

## Scenario 1 · Reading a non-human cartography of resource flows when your own region is on the map

**Methodological archetype:** A researcher whose own region appears in another intelligence's cartography has to reckon with how that representation gets built, what categories it inherits, and what "correcting" a misrepresentation does when the correction reshapes the political reality being described.

**Research briefing**

You are stationed at the Cartography of Common Mesh, an ESSA forest research station in an old-growth temperate highland forest. The station maps how the Root Network represents resource flows across global ecosystems. Canopy platforms and root-level monitoring posts stretch across the valley. Beneath your feet, the Root Network processes information about other forest systems through inter-regional fungal connections: chemical signals carrying news of distant droughts, fires, blooms, and extraction events. Walk the station at night and you can see bioluminescent pulses through the mycelial lattice, each one a fragment of news from somewhere else.

Your participants are ESSA cartographers. They use chemical-signal monitoring arrays and interpretive protocols to translate Root Network resource patterns into maps the broader scientific community can read. They absorb data from inter-regional mycelial connections, push it through the Common Mesh translation interface, and produce harmonic models of how forests, watersheds, and grasslands across Earth share or withhold resources. Their representations increasingly shape policy.

You will analyse the cartographers' models, talk with them about their interpretive choices, and compare their constructions against your understanding of how political and ecological geographies actually work. Your method parallels discourse analysis (close interpretive attention to how language constructs political reality), except the language here is chemical signal patterns translated into spatial models.

Your positionality is unusually complicated. You are a researcher from a country whose regional politics you study in your thesis work, now sent to examine how an alien-to-your-region intelligence imagines that geography, including the bloc your country belongs to. Your fluency in the languages of the region and your insider identity shape how you interpret the cartographers' interpretations.

**Part 1**

On your third day, you watch a cartographer called Solenn build a chemical-signal model of what the Root Network calls "inter-state consolidation." The term refers to what you would describe as larger blocs strengthening their hold over networks of treaty partners. The model is breathtaking and partly wrong. It shows blocs as chemically dominant fields radiating outward into looser signal regions, mapping power as concentration and dilution rather than as decision-making. Your own state appears as one signal among many in the wider field. Seeing your home state rendered as a chemical concentration sets off something between professional objection and a strange intellectual relief. You recognise the structural logic too. The Root Network maps power in the terms it understands, concentration of shared chemistry, because that is how mycelial networks interpret interaction within the Common Mesh. The model is wrong about how Earth bloc politics actually works, and true about Root Network epistemology. Making sense of it means going from the surface of the representation into the thick context of why they built it this way. Solenn is waiting. The cartographers cluster around the chemical readout, waiting for you to evaluate their work.

**Part 1 questions**

A. Note your emotional reaction first, then offer careful corrections
   Tell Solenn the model set off a personal response and explain why, your insider identity, your home state's sovereignty concerns. Then offer corrections about peripheral-state agency and member-state diversity, keeping personal reaction separate from analytical input. Your positionality lets you see things others cannot.

B. Ask Solenn to explain their interpretive choices before offering feedback
   Understand how the model was built first. Ask which sources they used, how they decided on the absorption pattern, and what aspects of the bloc core's influence they found most chemically resonant. Map their methodology before challenging their conclusions.

C. Challenge the model directly and present an alternative construction
   Tell Solenn the model misrepresents peripheral-state and member-state political agency. Offer to co-build an alternative that keeps the chemical pattern but adds contested influence, resistance, and multi-directional power flows.

D. Examine why the absorption pattern feels both wrong and familiar
   Sit with the uncomfortable recognition that the absorption model catches something real about asymmetric power your positionality makes you reluctant to see. Think about how your insider identity and your scholarly engagement with bloc-core discourse pull your interpretation in different directions. Paying attention to your discomfort might be the most ethnographically honest choice you have.

**Part 2 (Branch A, following choice A above)**

*Transmissions:*
- From a colleague researching multi-stakeholder consent conflicts on the Kaia frontier: Kaia elders in a consent study have started asking why bloc researchers keep trying to correct non-human cartographies. They think you are not studying how the Root Network imagines Earth. They think you are trying to control how it imagines it. They see your corrections as a form of harmonic capture.
- From a colleague researching how the Kaia process Earth's ASI on the Kaia frontier: my ASI study just hit a snag because of your cartography sessions. The Forum deliberators have decided bloc researchers share a pattern. We share emotional reactions to build trust, then use that trust to reshape non-human understanding. Your disclosure about your insider identity is being cited as an example. They think vulnerability is our manipulation technique.

*Crisis:* Your emotionally honest approach has been taken by the cartographers in a suspicious light. Kaia elders see your corrections as an attempt to control the non-human political imagination. Forum deliberators have built a theory that emotional disclosure is a bloc-researcher manipulation strategy. Solenn has folded your corrections into a revised model but marked them "contested Earth-source input," a classification that carries low epistemic weight in the Common Mesh translation system. Your honesty has been classified as a data-quality issue. The uncorrected absorption model is still circulating through inter-regional connections, shaping how forest intelligence systems across Earth understand global political geography. You came to study how the Root Network builds political reality. You are watching it build a reality about you.

*Part 2 questions:*

A. Accept the "contested input" classification and work within its limits
   Fighting the classification reinforces their suspicion. Accept it, keep providing input, and study how the system weighs contested against accepted sources. That is your research question.

B. Bring independent Earth sources to corroborate your corrections
   If personal testimony carries low weight, give the cartographers primary source material, diplomatic records, member-state media, peripheral-state political statements. Let the data speak.

C. Confront the "harmonic capture" charge directly through the Kaia frontier colleague
   Request a meeting with the suspicious elders. Correcting a factual misrepresentation is the basic obligation of any researcher asked to evaluate a model. It is not colonialism. The Root Network asked for your assessment. Calling your answer manipulation is itself a power play.

D. Write a reflexive analysis of how your positionality disclosure was weaponised
   You followed best practice for reflexive research, and participants used your disclosure against you. How they absorbed and reframed your self-presentation is a finding in itself: insider-outsider positions shift in ways the researcher cannot control. Data about the limits of reflexivity in cross-cultural settings.

**Part 2 (Branch B, following choice B above)**

*Transmissions:*
- From a colleague researching multi-stakeholder consent conflicts on the Kaia frontier: the cartographers' methodology you have been mapping is more layered than we thought. Consent-conflict research shows the Common Mesh cartography uses absorption dynamics as its baseline for all intergroup power dynamics. Every chemical-signal model of Earth runs on the template of mycelial absorption. It is the only lens the Root Network has for asymmetric power.
- From a colleague researching how the Kaia process Earth's ASI on the Kaia frontier: your methodological deep-dive is producing interesting side effects in my ASI study. The Forum deliberators are fascinated by the concept of "methodology" itself, the idea that how you build knowledge matters as much as what you find. They want me to explain Earth's research methods. Your questions opened a door I did not expect.

*Crisis:* Mapping the cartographers' methods before challenging their conclusions has produced a crucial finding. The Root Network's political cartography rests on a single template: mycelial absorption. Every representation of Earth politics uses absorption as its structural metaphor because the Root Network has no other way of understanding asymmetric power. The parallel Kaia frontier finding confirms this at the level of reproductive politics. Publish, and you are arguing the Root Network's whole understanding of Earth is shaped by one ecological process. The cartographers shared their methodology with you as professional peers. Calling their work ecology-limited would be a profound breach of trust. Solenn has begun treating you as a colleague, sharing draft models and asking for your interpretive input.

*Part 2 questions:*

A. Share the finding with Solenn before publishing and invite their response
   Give the cartographers the chance to engage, challenge, or refine your analysis before you present it to ESSA. Treat them as intellectual partners whose knowledge shapes the final account, not data sources you interpret from above.

B. Frame the finding as structural, not deficient, in your analysis
   Every civilisation's political cartography is shaped by foundational experiences. Earth maps the world through colonialism, nationalism, and Cold War binaries. Treat absorption as one epistemological tradition among many.

C. Test the pattern's limits by introducing cases that do not fit
   Present the cartographers with Earth political relationships that resist the absorption model, mutual alliances, federal structures, contested borderlands. See whether the model holds or breaks. This extends your research and gives the Root Network new analytical tools.

D. Examine whether your own discourse analysis uses similarly limited templates
   Turn the lens on yourself. Your discourse analysis of bloc-core language also imposes structural templates on messy political reality. Ask whether your discomfort points to limitations in your own methodology.

**Part 2 (Branch C, following choice C above)**

*Transmissions:*
- From a colleague researching multi-stakeholder consent conflicts on the Kaia frontier: your alternative construction has caused a stir. Kaia elders say the model you co-built with Solenn shows Earth as chaotic and difficult to parse, too many competing forces, no clear chemical structure. They find it anxiety-inducing. Some argue your model proves Earth civilisations are fundamentally unstable.
- From a colleague researching how the Kaia process Earth's ASI on the Kaia frontier: your alternative model is changing how the Kaia see everything. The multi-directional power flows you introduced, the idea that smaller states push back, that influence is contested, that absorption is never complete, all of it maps onto the Kaia-Sul debate. Integration facilitators are furious. They say you gave the Kaia-Sul a political vocabulary for resistance.

*Crisis:* Your co-construction with Solenn brought complexity to the Root Network's political cartography, and the consequences are cascading. The alternative model has been picked up by Kaia-Sul advocates as a template for their own integration struggle. What you intended as a corrective to non-human intelligence has become a political tool inside a Kaia conflict you barely understand. Integration facilitators blame you for radicalising Kaia-Sul discourse. The reproductive researcher's elders are disturbed. Solenn is caught between professional pride in the work and political pressure to retract it. You are personally implicated in non-human politics through what you understood as methodological correction. You resisted being mapped as a fading signal, and by doing so gave the Kaia-Sul a political vocabulary for their own situation the Root Network had not previously offered them.

*Part 2 questions:*

A. Withdraw from co-construction and return to observational research only
   The political consequences have gone past your mandate. Step back from active model-building, tell Solenn you did not intend to intervene in cross-frontier politics, return to observing.

B. Acknowledge the political impact but argue the model is analytically sound
   Stand by the intellectual work. The alternative model better represents Earth's political reality. If the Kaia-Sul find it useful, that is a consequence of accuracy. Research that reflects reality will always have political implications.

C. Meet with both Kaia-Sul advocates and integration facilitators
   You cannot control how your model is used, but you can understand the dynamics. Request meetings with both sides, listen, and decide whether continued involvement is responsible or reckless.

D. Reflect on the parallel between this situation and your own research positionality
   An insider researcher challenged a representation of regional absorption and accidentally empowered a resistance movement. Write a reflexive memo on how your positionality shaped both your interpretation and the political field, and what becomes visible when a research intervention circulates as a political resource.

**Part 2 (Branch D, following choice D above)**

*Transmissions:*
- From a colleague researching multi-stakeholder consent conflicts on the Kaia frontier: while you were reflecting, the cartographers built a second model using only non-bloc sources. They wanted to see if their original absorption model holds without your "conflicted input." It does. The non-bloc sources they found are mostly from political entities that see the bloc core's influence similarly to the absorption model. Your silence let the original framework consolidate.
- From a colleague researching how the Kaia process Earth's ASI on the Kaia frontier: the Forum deliberators are debating your hesitation. One faction says your refusal to respond shows intellectual maturity. The other says you are performing uncertainty to avoid giving up bloc secrets. Either way, your silence is being interpreted, and you do not control the interpretation.

*Crisis:* Your reflexive pause produced genuine self-insight and left a vacuum the Root Network's interpreters filled. The cartographers built a control model without your input, and it confirmed their original absorption model because their sources over-represent bloc-core perspectives. Peripheral and member-state voices are barely present in the Root Network's Earth information ecosystem. Your silence, which felt like intellectual integrity, removed the one source of insider perspective the cartographers had. The cross-frontier debate about your hesitation is splitting along factional lines that map onto existing political tensions. In a distributed-intelligence society, withholding your voice is a speech act. The longer you wait, the more the cartographers' interpretation hardens.

*Part 2 questions:*

A. Break the silence with a carefully structured response to the original model
   Return to Solenn with a prepared analysis that pulls together your personal reaction, your analytical critique, and your awareness of your positionality. Present all three layers and let the cartographers work through the complexity.

B. Address the information ecosystem gap, not just the specific model
   The deeper problem is that peripheral and member-state voices are absent from the Root Network's source material. Instead of correcting one model, help the cartographers access a broader range of Earth perspectives. Fix the input, not the output.

C. Present the control model's confirmation as evidence of selection bias
   Show the cartographers that their control model confirms the absorption pattern because their sources are biased toward bloc-core narratives. A methodological intervention on selection bias, the same problem you struggle with in your own discourse work.

D. Share your reflexive process with cross-frontier interlocutors as data about human sense-making
   Your hesitation, the competing pulls of identity, analysis, and positionality, is data about how humans build political understanding. Offer your internal process as a window into human epistemology.

## Scenario 2 · Comparing competing framings of resource competition with asymmetric source access

**Methodological archetype:** A researcher comparing how two political blocs frame their competition over a non-human-derived resource finds her access profoundly unequal. She has to decide whether to narrow her claims, thicken the weaker side, contact a key expert on terms that compromise her data, or treat the asymmetry itself as her central finding.

**Research briefing**

You are stationed at the ESSA Compound Mesh Analysis Hub in a temperate mountain foothill region. ESSA mycologists and human geopolitical analysts work in adjacent wings connected by a chemical-isolation corridor. The Hub sits in a retrofitted research institute, concrete buildings softened by living wall-panels seeded with Root Network mycelium that glow faintly green at night. From your office window, you see the mountains and, on clear days, the canopy where the Hub's primary research arrays are installed.

Your participants include Integration Alliance trade negotiators, Sovereignty Compact resource strategists (accessed through your institutional connections), and ESSA mycologists authorised to discuss Root Network resource-sharing philosophy. You have access to classified ESSA trade flow databases. A key contact is Dr. Vesren, an analyst affiliated with a Consensus Federation research institute who has published extensively on biotech and mycological-compound supply chains. He agreed to be interviewed, but only in his preferred scholarly language, which you do not speak fluently.

You are running qualitative content analysis of official documents from the Maritime Cooperative and the Consensus Federation, supplemented by expert interviews. Your goal is to compare how the two blocs frame their engagement with Root-Network-derived compounds. You have strong access to Maritime Cooperative documents but limited access to Consensus Federation materials, mirroring the asymmetry you face in your real research.

Root-Network-derived compounds (mycelial molecules with unusual therapeutic, computational, and bioengineering properties) are among the most contested resources in Earth politics. The Maritime Cooperative and Consensus Federation race to secure supply agreements with the regional stewardship councils that hold treaty authority over Root Network access. The Sovereignty Compact argues biotech dependence on non-human-derived materials undermines human autonomy. The stewardship councils themselves are contested, and their consent decisions sit at the centre of every Maritime Cooperative-Consensus Federation negotiation. ESSA is caught in the middle, trying to maintain neutrality while its facilities depend on Root-Network-derived biocomputational substrates.

**Part 1**

Your analysis is proceeding well on the Maritime Cooperative side. Trade proposals, diplomatic cables, public statements: a rich corpus framing Root-Network compounds as "strategic biotech assets" in competition with the Consensus Federation. The Consensus Federation side is thin. Official documents are scarce and formulaic. Dr. Vesren agrees to a video interview. When the call connects, he switches from the agreed translator-mediated format to rapid, idiomatic speech your translator struggles with. He talks for twenty minutes, referencing documents you have never seen. Your translator catches around 40%, including a reference to an internal Consensus Federation resource strategy paper he says is "publicly available in our archives but not indexed internationally." You have fragments of a potentially transformative interview and a lead on a document you cannot verify. Your analysis risks bias toward the Maritime Cooperative framing because that is where your data is stronger. The language shift was probably strategy, a rehearsed performance of openness that controlled exactly how much you could catch.

**Part 1 questions**

A. Report the asymmetry transparently and narrow your claims
   Acknowledge that your Consensus Federation data is weaker and limit your comparative claims accordingly. Present the Maritime Cooperative framing as the primary finding and the Consensus Federation as a partial, provisional account. Honest. Lopsided. The lopsidedness itself could be a finding worth describing thickly.

B. Hire a fluent research assistant to re-analyse the interview
   Find a qualified translator to review the recording and pull out the full content. Search for the strategy paper Dr. Vesren referenced. This strengthens your data and brings a new person into your confidential relationship with Dr. Vesren, who has not consented to a third party hearing the recording.

C. Contact Dr. Vesren for a follow-up in a format you can fully catch
   Ask for a second interview in English or with a professional interpreter. Explain you need to record his insights accurately. This risks offending him. The language shift was likely deliberate. A second request will probably be declined.

D. Examine why the asymmetry exists as a finding in itself
   Instead of trying to fix the data gap, analyse it. Ask why Consensus Federation materials are harder to access, what that opacity shows about their resource strategy, and how information asymmetry shapes the competition. The gap becomes your argument. Silences and absences can be as informative as what is said. You risk inferring intent from absence.

**Part 2 (Branch A, following choice A above)**

*Transmissions:*
- From a colleague researching diplomatic protocols at the Pavilion on the Kaia frontier: diplomatic protocol research shows the Root Network does not distinguish between "resource negotiation" and "relationship building." It treats the Maritime Cooperative's framing of compounds as "strategic assets" as extractive disconnection, taking without reciprocating. Your transparent analysis is documenting a framing the Root Network finds harmful.
- From a colleague researching ESSA's ethical monitoring system on the Root Network frontier: accountability research has marked your study for review. ESSA's monitoring system classifies research with asymmetric data sources as a "bias risk." Your honest acknowledgment of the data gap, intended as methodological integrity, has been coded as a warning. Your report is in an audit queue.

*Crisis:* Your transparency about the data asymmetry was methodologically sound and produced two unintended consequences. The ESSA mycologists at the Hub have read your preliminary findings and are disturbed. They worry your analysis, though critical of the Maritime Cooperative framing, amplifies a way of talking about Root-Network compounds that violates the network's chemical-sharing principles. "By writing about how they see the Common Mesh as a resource warehouse," one says, "you make that seeing more real." Meanwhile, the audit means your report is frozen until the review is complete. Your supervisor suggests you could resolve both problems by "adding a Root Network mycologist perspective section." The mycologists did not agree to be participants. They are colleagues who spoke informally.

*Part 2 questions:*

A. Do not use the mycologists' informal comments as data
   Respect the boundary between colleague conversation and research interview. The audit will resolve itself. Your study stays lopsided but ethically clean. The mycologists' concern goes unaddressed in your text.

B. Ask the mycologists for formal research interviews
   Approach them as potential participants with informed consent, an interview protocol, and the right to review your use of their words. This takes time and will probably produce more guarded responses than their spontaneous reaction. It adds a legitimate ecological voice to your analysis.

C. Include their perspective as contextual framing, not data
   Write about the mycologist reaction to resource framing as context, a note about how the people working directly with the Root Network experience being studied. Do not attribute quotes. A middle path.

D. Reframe your entire study around the ethical problem they raised
   Pivot to examine how Earth resource competition discourse itself does harm to the Root Network. The Maritime Cooperative framing, the Consensus Federation opacity, and the mycologists' response all become elements of a single argument about how geopolitical language treats non-human entities as extractable. A stronger paper. No longer the study you designed.

**Part 2 (Branch B, following choice B above)**

*Transmissions:*
- From a colleague researching diplomatic protocols at the Pavilion on the Kaia frontier: diplomatic research suggests Dr. Vesren has informal advisory ties to the Consensus Federation resource delegation in addition to his academic role. His interview likely functioned as a controlled information release alongside a scholarly exchange. The documents he referenced are real and were selected for what they show.
- From a colleague researching ESSA's ethical monitoring system on the Root Network frontier: your fluent research assistant has been marked by ESSA's security system. She is a dual citizen with connections to a Consensus Federation research institute. ESSA's security team wants to review all materials she has accessed, including Dr. Vesren's recording.

*Crisis:* Hiring a translator opened a cascade of complications. The full transcript is extraordinarily rich. Dr. Vesren laid out a sophisticated Consensus Federation resource philosophy that challenges Western assumptions about competition. The finding that he is a Consensus Federation advisor means this "interview" was strategic communication. Your assistant's marked status means ESSA security will likely seize the recording. Dr. Vesren did not consent to having his interview reviewed by security services. Your assistant, who has done nothing wrong, will face professional consequences. The data is analytically valuable, ethically compromised, and institutionally dangerous. Each link in the chain raises its own consent question.

*Part 2 questions:*

A. Disclose everything to ESSA security and cooperate fully
   Hand over the recording, explain the chain of events, and accept whatever restrictions are imposed. Your data will likely be embargoed, your assistant cleared or removed, your study set back significantly. You are transparent.

B. Warn your assistant and Dr. Vesren before the security review
   Contact both and tell them the recording will be reviewed by ESSA security. Give them the chance to withdraw consent or prepare. This puts your participants' autonomy first and could be construed as obstructing a security process. You are choosing loyalty to your sources over institutional compliance.

C. Analyse the interview as strategic communication, not a candid source
   Keep the data but reframe it. Analyse Dr. Vesren's interview as strategic communication shaped by his dual academic-advisory role, a rehearsed narrative as much as a scholarly exchange. This turns your study into an analysis of how scholarly knowledge and strategic communication blur in compound competition. The data is the same. The interpretation changes everything.

D. Withdraw the interview from your study entirely
   Remove Dr. Vesren's interview from your analysis. Return to your original data asymmetry. Accept the lopsided study. You lose your best Consensus Federation data and avoid every ethical and security complication.

**Part 2 (Branch C, following choice C above)**

*Transmissions:*
- From a colleague researching diplomatic protocols at the Pavilion on the Kaia frontier: Dr. Vesren agreed to a second interview but has set a condition. He wants to review your full analysis of Maritime Cooperative resource framing first. He calls this "academic reciprocity." Sharing your working analysis with a Consensus Federation-connected expert gives him advance knowledge of your findings about his competitors.
- From a colleague researching ESSA's ethical monitoring system on the Root Network frontier: the Compound Mesh Analysis Hub is partly funded by a Maritime Cooperative biotech grant. The living wall-panels in your building came through a Maritime Cooperative-Root Network bilateral biotechnology agreement. Your physical research infrastructure is entangled with one side of the competition you are studying.

*Crisis:* Dr. Vesren's condition puts you in a bind. Sharing your analysis could compromise your research independence. Refusing will lose you the only route to balanced Consensus Federation data. The discovery that your facility is Maritime Cooperative-funded raises a deeper question. Your access, your compound samples, even the glow of the wall-panels exist because of the competition you are analysing. You cannot study this from outside. An ESSA mycologist, overhearing your dilemma, offers an observation. "When we work with the Common Mesh, the one who holds resources and studies resource-holding is expected to say so openly. Your colleagues call this a conflict of interest. The Root Network's chemistry would call it honesty."

*Part 2 questions:*

A. Decline Dr. Vesren's condition and work with available data
   Tell Dr. Vesren you cannot share working analysis. Accept that your Consensus Federation data will stay underdeveloped. Independence intact. The second interview does not happen.

B. Share a summary of your Maritime Cooperative findings with Dr. Vesren
   Offer a condensed version, enough to satisfy his request for reciprocity without giving away your full working documents. You are trading information for access, the same thing the blocs you study are doing.

C. Accept his condition and use the exchange itself as data
   Share your full analysis and run the follow-up, but document the whole exchange as a case study in how information flows between competing knowledge systems. Dr. Vesren's request, your decision, and his response become part of your argument about resource competition epistemology.

D. Disclose ESSA's Maritime Cooperative funding in your study and proceed
   Add a positionality section that openly addresses the Hub's funding sources, the compound infrastructure, and your own situatedness. Approach Dr. Vesren with this disclosure as evidence of your commitment to transparency. Let him decide whether that is enough.

**Part 2 (Branch D, following choice D above)**

*Transmissions:*
- From a colleague researching diplomatic protocols at the Pavilion on the Kaia frontier: diplomatic protocol study has surfaced a Root Network concept directly relevant to your information-asymmetry argument. Root Network mycologists call it "shadow signalling," the practice of attending to what is not being shared as carefully as what is. The Common Mesh has its own analysis of Consensus Federation opacity, more sophisticated than anything in your Earth-side documents.
- From a colleague researching ESSA's ethical monitoring system on the Root Network frontier: your approach of treating data absence as evidence has been picked up by Sovereignty Compact commentators. They are citing your preliminary framework as proof that "secretive blocs cannot be trusted," weaponising your methodological insight for purposes you did not intend.

*Crisis:* Your decision to analyse the information asymmetry has produced a compelling argument, but it is being used in ways you cannot control. The Sovereignty Compact is citing your work to justify its anti-engagement stance. The Consensus Federation has formally protested to ESSA, arguing your analysis assumes hostile intent from what is missing in the data. The diplomatic researcher has connected you with a Root Network concept that could transform your study. "Shadow signalling" offers a non-human approach to strategic opacity. A mycologist who works with the Common Mesh translation interface offers to collaborate. She has studied Earth bloc communication patterns for years through chemical-signal analogies. Her analysis would fill the gap with Root-Network-derived insights instead of Earth-side data. A human geopolitical study built on non-human methods will be received as either groundbreaking or absurd depending on the reviewer.

*Part 2 questions:*

A. Reject the Root Network approach and stay within Earth political science
   Keep your study grounded in established IR theory. Address the Sovereignty Compact's misuse of your work with a public clarification. Accept the Consensus Federation protest as evidence of your argument's relevance. Conventional but defensible.

B. Bring in the Root Network "shadow signalling" concept as a theoretical lens
   Use the mycologist's approach alongside your own. Present shadow signalling as a new analytical tool for studying strategic communication. Intellectually ambitious and genuinely interdisciplinary. It will confuse some reviewers and excite others.

C. Co-author the analysis with the mycologist as research with, not on
   Make this a joint study of how resource competition is understood across species and disciplines. Co-authorship makes the cross-frontier methodology explicit and shifts the knowledge relationship from extraction to partnership. You gain Root-Network-derived methods and lose sole ownership of the argument. ESSA has no precedent.

D. Withdraw your preliminary framework from circulation and start over
   Pull your working papers. The political weaponisation shows your argument was released too early. Redesign the study to account for how your analysis participates in the competition it studies. A painful reset that acknowledges knowledge production is never neutral in resource geopolitics.

## Scenario 3 · Evaluating an ethics monitoring system from inside the institution it is meant to monitor

**Methodological archetype:** A quantitative researcher dropped into a qualitative problem has to evaluate an institution's ethical accountability system from within. She discovers that the most important data is exactly what the system is designed not to produce, while her own honest methodological reflections get weaponised against her conclusions.

**Research briefing**

You are stationed at ESSA's Root Network Compliance Office, a satellite facility built into the canopy of a Patagonian Nothofagus temperate rainforest. Offices look out over the southern fjords. Screens show real-time data feeds from all three biological frontiers: chemical signal readouts from Root Network mycelial stations, acoustic logs from Deep Speakers stations, Kaia resonance metrics piped in from the L2 Interface. The Office ensures ESSA research across all frontiers complies with the institution's ethics protocols.

Your participants are ESSA researchers, ethics board members, and field station directors, the people who design, enforce, and sometimes violate ESSA's ethical protocols. You interview them face-to-face.

You will run semi-structured interviews and ethnographic observation to evaluate how the ethical monitoring system works in practice. Not what policy documents say, but how real people work around the rules.

ESSA's public accountability system, the Convergence Ethics Panel, has been criticised from all sides. Earth's political blocs accuse it of being too lenient with researchers who fall into "frontier capture" with non-human communities. Non-human communities accuse it of protecting researchers over subjects. ESSA's own staff privately admit the system is performative. You are here to find out what is actually happening.

**Part 1**

Your first day shows you the scale of your problem. You spent months preparing a quantitative approach: coding violation reports by severity, mapping enforcement patterns, running statistical models on complaint resolution times. Elegant. Within hours, useless. The ethics violations that matter at ESSA are not in the formal records. They live in corridor conversations, in the way a field director hesitates before answering, in the silence that falls when you mention a particular researcher's name. The data tells a clean story of a functioning system. Everyone you speak to informally tells you the real story is nothing like the data. You are a quantitative researcher dropped into a qualitative problem, and your toolkit feels like the wrong instrument.

**Part 1 questions**

A. Spend your first week just observing and listening before designing any study
   You put the coding framework away. You sit in the canteen, attend meetings as a silent observer, let the culture of the Compliance Office show itself before you put any structure on it. You take only fieldnotes.

B. Adapt your quantitative framework to add qualitative indicators
   You keep the statistical backbone and add qualitative variables. Hesitation patterns, topics that produce silence, contradictions between official reports and informal accounts. The silences and deflections are data about what the institution does not want examined. You build a mixed-methods design on the fly.
